# Phase 33 — Civilization Crises & Policy Choices

Phase 33 makes the Phase 32 civilization produce strategic dilemmas with persistent civic consequences.

## Civic ledger

The city now tracks four non-learning civic meters:

- Stability
- Innovation
- Capacity
- Public trust

These meters are intentionally separate from mastery, readiness, hidden-test results, and career scoring.

## Pressure loop

Demonstrated growth creates civic pressure. Mastery, retention, district evolution, projects, repository reviews, landmarks, and Interview Days increment pressure. At the threshold, a deterministic crisis appears.

Initial crises include:

- Compute Grid Brownout
- Civic Data Integrity Incident
- Population Growth Overload
- Research Translation Bottleneck

Recent crises are rotated so the same dilemma is not immediately repeated.

## Strategic choices

Every crisis has a baseline response plus stronger responses unlocked by the civilization the player actually built. Unlocks may depend on district levels, review/project/retention evidence, or landmarks.

No choice is universally best. Responses trade stability, innovation, capacity, and trust against each other. This is meant to create an actual civilization strategy layer rather than a disguised quiz.

## Learning authority boundary

Civic crises never remove mastery, fail a coding challenge, lower Interview Day scores, damage retention evidence, revoke career progress, or erase earned resources. Engineering competence expands strategic options; policy decisions affect only the civic simulation.

## Persistence

Civic meters, active crises, pressure, and recent policy history are stored locally. Crisis resolution emits telemetry and micro-feedback events for the existing game-feel systems.
